eWash — Laundry Management SaaS

Product Specification · Data Model · Clickable Mockup  |  v0.1 Draft · July 2026 · Kenya-first

1. Executive Summary

eWash (working title) is a multi-tenant SaaS platform sold to laundry businesses in Kenya and East Africa. Each subscribing laundry (a tenant) gets a configurable service catalog, a pricing builder, an order-processing pipeline, M-Pesa & cash payments, and customer notifications — without writing code or calling a developer when prices or services change.

The core product bet: laundries differ wildly in what they sell and how they price it, but not in how work flows through the shop. So the catalog and pricing engine are fully tenant-configurable, while the order lifecycle is a shared, opinionated workflow.

3. Multi-Tenancy & SaaS Model

  • Tenant = one laundry business. All catalog, orders, customers, and staff are scoped to a tenant. Strict row-level isolation (tenant_id on every table).
  • Branches. A tenant has ≥1 branch. Orders, staff, and cash registers belong to a branch; the catalog is tenant-wide with optional per-branch price overrides (Phase 2).
  • Subscription plans (eWash's own revenue): e.g. Starter (1 branch, 2 staff), Growth (3 branches, unlimited staff, WhatsApp), Pro (API, per-branch pricing, loyalty). Billed monthly via M-Pesa or card.
  • Tenant onboarding wizard: business name → branch → seed a template catalog (typical Kenyan laundry services pre-configured) → set rates → invite staff → go live in under 15 minutes. Template catalogs are the key to activation.

4. Service Catalog & Pricing Builder CORE

The differentiator. A service is not a name with a price — it is a name with a pricing strategy, composed in an admin UI ("the builder").

4.1 Structure

  • Category — grouping for the UI and reports: Washing, Ironing, Dry Cleaning, Special Items, Household.
  • Service — the sellable thing: "Wash & Fold (per kg)", "Duvet Wash", "Iron per Item", "Suit Dry Clean".
  • Pricing strategy — one of four models, each with its own configuration:
ModelConfigurationExample
PER_KGRate per kg, optional minimum charge, optional tier breaksKES 150/kg, min KES 500; 10+ kg → KES 130/kg
PER_ITEMBase price, optional variants by attributeDuvet: Single 600 / Double 800 / King 1,000
FLATOne price regardless of quantity"Bag wash" KES 1,200
TIEREDQuantity/weight bands with band prices1–5 kg: 800 flat; 6–10 kg: 1,400 flat
  • Modifiers (stack on any strategy): minimum charge, express surcharge (% or fixed, e.g. +50% for same-day), branch override (Phase 2), customer-tier discount (Phase 2).

4.2 Add-ons / Riders CORE

An add-on is just another service that is allowed to attach to a parent order line. Ironing exists once as a service; configuration decides where it can attach and at what price:

  • Attachment rule: Ironing → attachable to [Wash & Fold, Duvet Wash].
  • Bundled price override: standalone ironing is KES 70/kg, but 50/kg when riding on a wash line.
  • Quantity inheritance: if parent and rider share a unit (kg↔kg), the rider inherits the parent's quantity by default; the attendant can override (e.g. iron only 10 of 25 items).
  • The same mechanism covers stain treatment, softener, folding, express handling — no special cases.

4.3 Pricing engine resolution order

  1. Resolve service + variant → base strategy rate
  2. Apply quantity/weight → raw line amount (tiers applied here)
  3. Apply minimum charge (line level)
  4. Price each attached rider the same way (using bundled override if set)
  5. Apply order-level modifiers: express surcharge, discounts/promos
  6. Snapshot everything. The order stores the resolved unit prices; later catalog changes never alter existing orders.
Rule that prevents 90% of disputes: prices are locked at customer confirmation, not at delivery. The order stores a full price snapshot with a breakdown that is sent to the customer.

5. Order Lifecycle & Workflow

Key operational reality: for per-kg services the price is unknown at drop-off — it is discovered when the bag is weighed at the shop. The lifecycle is built around a quote → confirm step.

StageWhoWhat happens
1. IntakeAttendant (or customer pickup request)Order created, customer identified (phone = ID), bag received, estimate optionally given. Pickup orders get a rider assignment.
2. AssessmentAttendantWeighing & itemization: per-kg lines weighed, individual items counted with variants (duvet: double), riders attached. Pricing engine computes final total. Tags printed per item/bundle.
3. ConfirmationSystem → CustomerSMS/WhatsApp with itemized quote + M-Pesa STK push (if pay-on-confirm policy). Customer approves; price snapshot locked. Walk-ins confirm on the spot.
4. ProcessingOperatorsPipeline stages: Sorting → Washing → Drying → Ironing → Packaging. Status tracked per order (MVP) with per-line stage flags where riders exist.
5. ReadySystem → Customer"Your order is ready" notification; unpaid orders get a payment reminder + STK push.
6. Delivery / CollectionAttendant or RiderHandover verified against tags; collected-by recorded. Order → Closed.

Payment states (independent of order status)

UNPAID → PARTIALLY_PAID → PAID, plus REFUNDED. Methods: M-Pesa STK push (Daraja API), M-Pesa manual code entry, cash (registered against attendant + branch till), credit for corporate accounts (Phase 2). Partial payments are first-class — deposits at drop-off are common.

Garment tagging & traceability

Every order gets a short human code (e.g. WK-0247); each item/bundle gets a printed tag (QR/barcode-ready). Lost-item claims are the #1 trust-killer for laundries; per-item traceability is a selling point of the software itself.

Every price-affecting action (manual discount, void, refund, catalog change) is written to an immutable audit log — owners are trusting employees with cash, and the audit trail is a top-3 buying reason.

7.1 Custom roles & granular policies CORE

The table above ships as role templates. Underneath sits a granular policy catalog: orders.create, orders.discount, orders.void, orders.advance, payments.receive, payments.refund, catalog.edit, expenses.create, finance.view, users.manage, and so on. An admin can build custom roles from these policies, and additionally grant or revoke individual policies per user — a per-user override that beats the role template (an explicit deny always wins). Effective permission = override if present, else the role's policy. Overrides are visibly flagged in the UI so exceptions never hide, and every change is audit-logged. Rule of thumb: roles for the common case, overrides for exceptions ("Peter is an operator, but he may also create orders").

8. Reporting & Analytics

  • Daily register: orders in/out, cash vs M-Pesa collected, per attendant — reconciles the till.
  • Revenue by service/category: is money in per-kg volume or duvets? Informs pricing.
  • Operational: average turnaround time, orders overdue in a stage, uncollected-ready orders (cash tied up on the shelf).
  • Customers: repeat rate, top customers, lapsed customers (Phase 2 win-back SMS).

9. Basic Accounting & Profit/Loss CORE

Deliberately simple: cash-basis income and expenses, not double-entry bookkeeping. The goal is that an owner can answer "did I make money this month?" without an accountant. Data exports as CSV for whoever files the taxes.

9.1 Revenue (automatic)

  • Revenue is recognized when an order is closed (delivered / collected) — automatically, no data entry. An order sitting on the shelf is not income yet; profit is computed strictly from closed orders. Broken down by service category, branch, and payment method.
  • Payments are tracked separately for cash flow: a "billed vs collected" line exposes closed-but-unpaid orders (receivables to chase) so revenue and cash never get confused.
  • Deductions are visible, not hidden: manual discounts, refunds, and zero-priced re-wash orders each appear as a line, so leakage is measurable.

9.2 Expenses (light manual entry)

  • An expense entry is: date, category, amount, branch, paid via (cash/M-Pesa), note, optional receipt photo, recorded-by.
  • Default categories seeded per tenant: Detergents & supplies, Water, Electricity, Rent, Salaries & wages, Delivery/fuel, Equipment & repairs, Other. Owner can add categories.
  • Recurring expenses (rent, salaries): flag an entry as monthly and it auto-posts on its day with a confirmation nudge — this is what keeps the P&L honest with near-zero effort.
  • Attendants may record petty expenses (capped); everything is in the audit log; only the owner sees the P&L.

9.3 The P&L statement

One screen, per month/branch: Gross revenue − discounts & refunds = Net revenue; − expenses by category = Operating profit, with margin % and month-over-month comparison. That's the whole statement.

Explicit non-goals (v1): no double-entry ledger, no accruals or depreciation schedules, no VAT filing, no balance sheet. If a tenant outgrows this, CSV export feeds their accountant or a future QuickBooks-style integration — better than building bad accounting software inside a laundry product.

10. Edge Cases & Business Rules

  • Mixed orders: per-kg wash + 2 duvets + ironing rider on the wash line only — the line-item model handles this natively.
  • Re-wash / complaint: zero-priced redo order linked to the original; tracked so quality issues are measurable.
  • Weight disputes: weight recorded at assessment and included in the confirmation message before processing starts.
  • Rate changes: price snapshots make catalog edits safe; only new orders see new rates.
  • Uncollected orders: configurable policy — reminder cadence, optional storage fee after N days (a per-day FLAT service, again the same engine).
  • Offline tolerance: shops have unreliable connectivity; intake must queue locally (PWA with local cache) and sync.

11. Non-Functional Requirements

  • Tenant isolation enforced at the data layer; no cross-tenant reads ever.
  • Money stored in integer cents, KES default, currency field per tenant for regional expansion.
  • All status transitions and payments are append-only history, not overwrites.
  • Mobile-first UI — most attendants will use a phone or cheap tablet.
  • M-Pesa (Daraja) sandbox-first integration with idempotent callbacks.

customers.phone unique per tenantPhone number is the natural customer ID in this market; powers SMS, M-Pesa matching, and repeat-customer lookup at intake.
